# ChannelScope release `ryr1_mappings_v0.1`: residue mappings for RyR1

**What this release is.** Residue-by-residue mappings from human RyR1 (UniProt P21817, isoform 1, sequence version 3) to rabbit (P11716, sequence version 1), mouse (E9PZQ0, sequence version 1) and pig (P16960, sequence version 2) RyR1, and the map from the human gene's coding sequence (RefSeq NM_000540.3 on GRCh38) to the protein, base by base. Each human residue maps to one residue of the other species, or to "no correspondence". Each mapping comes from a global alignment, compared with a second, separately written aligner. Every row carries a reliability flag with its reasons, and an *ambiguous* flag where an alignment of equal score would pair it differently. Two search records say what searches of UniProt and of the PDB found for human RyR1 on 29 September 2026: two X-ray structures, 6UHI and 6UHS, whose chain A covers human residues 849 to 962, and no structure of the whole human channel. A search shows what it found on its date, not that nothing exists.

**Three checks compare with figures this release does not carry.** The codon map's check G6, and check A11 on the human-rabbit and the human-pig mappings, compare with tables from an earlier version of this project (July 2026), which is not public; check A7 compares the human-rabbit alignment's identity with a figure recorded earlier by a separate run of the same method. Each compares with an earlier computation from the same sequences, so each shows that two computations agree, not that either is right, and none can be re-run from public sources. Their four records are withheld, since each holds a local path or a person's name inside its content digest, and are summarised in `withheld_check_records.json`. Check A11's committed rule is that "boundaries are compared only at indels our two aligners agree on", which the code applies through the *ambiguous* flag. Of the 5,029 human residues in the earlier table's segments, 5,009 agree with it. At the other 20, all flagged *ambiguous*, this mapping differs from the earlier table, and the rule excuses the differences there. The check's record lists them under the name `not_compared_ambiguous`, though they were compared.

**Check A6 failed, and this is why.** A6 tests the human-rabbit mapping against ten published statements of which human residue corresponds to which rabbit residue. They were listed between 29 September and 3 October 2026, before the mapping was built. Nine were reproduced. The tenth, entry A6-03, comes from the methods of Iyer et al. 2020 (*Sci Adv* 6:eabb2964), which describe a rabbit R164C mutant as "equivalent to mutant R164C in humans", that is, human 164 to rabbit 164. The same paper's introduction gives another number: "mutations R163C (R164C in rabbit) in RyR1".

In the human sequence (UniProt P21817-1, sequence version 3), residue 164 is valine and the arginine is at 163. Human residues 159 to 168 read GEKVRVGDDI, and rabbit 160 to 169 read GEKVRVGDDL. So the rabbit arginine at 164 corresponds to human 163, as the introduction says. A later paper from the same laboratory gives the same pair: "RyR1 with the MH/CCD R163C mutation (rabbit R164C)" (Iyer et al. 2022, *PNAS* 119:e2122140119).

A6's list holds both statements of the 2020 paper as separate entries: A6-02 (163 to 164) and A6-03 (164 to 164). They name the same rabbit residue, and since the mapping is one-to-one, at most one could be reproduced. The list recorded that conflict when it was committed, on 29 September 2026. The mapping reproduces A6-02. At that point it is a clean pair: the nearest insertion or deletion is 159 columns away, its local identity is 0.952 over 21 pairs, and it is not flagged *ambiguous*, so none of the 256 alignments of equal score pairs human 163 or 164 differently. A6-03 would fail under any mapping, since the check also compares the stated amino acid with the sequence, and human 164 is not an arginine.

**What check A6's planted fault showed.** Each check was also run on a copy of its input with a fault planted in it. For A6, the fault moves every human residue from 4540 on one rabbit residue further. The record labels that control "detected", but here the label carries no information: A6 fails whenever any entry is not reproduced, and A6-03 cannot be, so a faulted run fails too, whatever the fault does. What shows that the fault was seen is the count of entries reproduced: 5 under the fault, against 9 in the real run.

No mapping value was changed because of the failure, and the pass rule was not rewritten after the run. This release carries A6's list, byte for byte as the check read it, with its record and a way to run check A6 alone (`a6/run_a6.py`), so that anyone can re-run the comparison.

**How a re-run will be read** was fixed on 11 October 2026, before any recorded re-run, and is given in full in this release's file `how_a_rerun_of_a6_is_read.md`. A re-run is this known case only if all four of these hold: A6's list has the same SHA-256 as in the first run; all ten rows of the result are identical to the first run's, field for field; the stretch of sequence it reports around the failing entry is identical; and the planted fault lowers the count of reproduced entries below the real run's. A known re-run is still reported as a failure, with this explanation. Any other result is news: it is investigated on both sides, as a possible error in the source or in ours, with neither presumed right.

**One more thing a reader should know.** Some residues are in an insertion or deletion only because of which alignment of equal score was kept: the one whose gaps fall as late in the sequence as possible, towards the C-terminus. Such residues are flagged *ambiguous*, since another alignment of equal score gives them a partner. They are six of the human-rabbit mapping's 17 indel residues (human 4500; rabbit 5, 1888 to 1890 and 3748), six of the human-mouse mapping's 23 and two of the human-pig mapping's 11.

**What it is not.** These mappings say which residues correspond, not what a change at any of them does. They support reading structures of rabbit, mouse and pig for human residues ("template use"). They are not evidence that a finding transfers from one protein to another. Agreement with an earlier computation shows agreement, not correctness.
